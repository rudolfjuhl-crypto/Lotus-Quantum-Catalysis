Quantum Simulation of the FeMoco Active Space: Resolving Low-Temperature 
Industrial Nitrogen Fixation via Distributed 288-Qubit Lotus Hexa QPU
================================================================================
Author: Rudolf Juhl | Location: Bensheim, Germany | Date: November 7, 2026
DOI-Registry: CERN / Zenodo Open-Science Network Reference

ABSTRACT:
Industrial nitrogen fixation via the classical Haber-Bosch process consumes 
approximately 2-3% of global energy annually and demands extreme thermal (500°C) 
and baric (200 bar) boundaries due to the stability of the dinitrogen triple bond. 
Conversely, biological nitrogenase enzymes achieve nitrogen cleavage under ambient 
conditions utilizing the transition-metal cluster iron-molybdenum cofactor 
(FeMoco: Fe7MoS9C). Modelling the strongly correlated d-orbital electronic states 
of the FeMoco active space generates an exponential scaling bottleneck that 
defeats classical supercomputers. This paper presents the quantum-driven 
simulation of the FeMoco configuration utilizing a distributed Variational Quantum 
Eigensolver (VQE) architecture mapped onto the 288-qubit Lotus Core-V3 Hexa 
superconducting processor network. By utilizing compiler-level Virtual-Z gate 
injections (theta = 8.39°), inter-die phase skews are completely neutralized, 
converging at an absolute electronic ground-state energy of -214.8365 Hartree. 
The resolved wavefunctions enable the structural blueprinting of synthetic, 
ambient-pressure industrial bio-catalysts.
